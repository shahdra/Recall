01 — The problem

Students forget most of what they study within days.

Passive re-reading feels productive and produces poor retention. The evidence-based remedy is active recall plus spaced repetition — quizzing yourself, with harder material resurfacing more often. Almost nobody does it, because hand-building flashcards is tedious and tracking what to review and when is a chore. Recall removes that friction.

Where these curves come from (and what's illustrative)

The decay shape is the published Ebbinghaus forgetting curve, drawn as R = floor + (1−floor)·e^(−t/S) — a fast-fading trace over a durable floor, which is what makes it steep in the first days and shallow later. Calibrated to the familiar landmarks: about 77% recall after a day and ~20% after two weeks. Each review lengthens stability and lifts the floor. The curves are illustrative — not measured from Recall's production traffic.

The review days are real: they are what services/study-mcp/sm2.py actually schedules for a card graded 5 (perfect recall) every time — FIRST_INTERVALS[5] = 4, SECOND_INTERVALS[5] = 9, then interval × ease_factor with ease starting at 2.5.

Upload anything

Paste text or drop a PDF. The Card-Generator turns it into {front, back, topic} flashcards. Raw files go to S3; large files never touch the database.

Quiz by voice or text

Answer by typing or speaking — Deepgram Nova transcribes. The Grader judges the answer, explains why, and assigns an SM-2 quality grade 0–5.

It remembers you

A per-learner profile — weak topics, accuracy, streak — is read at session start, injected into the tutor's prompt, and rewritten after every session. The tutor adapts across sessions.

Every hop, in order

    Sub-agents are not services

    One LLM, three prompted roles. generate_cards(material, LLM) and grade_answer(...) are ordinary Python calls inside the tutor-agent process, sharing its model object — which is why they are drawn nested in that box, not out on the network.

    The ReAct loop runs on /chat only

    /decks, /session/* and /transcribe are straight-line Python — they call exactly what they need, in order. Only free-form chat hands control to the hand-written loop, capped at 8 model calls.

    SM-2 never reaches the model

    The Grader returns a quality 0–5. The scheduling arithmetic lives in sm2.py and runs inside grade_card. Neither the endpoint nor the LLM ever computes a date — the schedule is deterministic and testable.

    03 — Alerting & monitoring

    "Healthy" means the tutor is actually teaching.

    Process-up is not health. Alongside HTTP latency and error rate, Recall exports its own series — and the alerts are built so a rolling deploy never pages anyone. Inject a failure below and watch the state machine work.

    Why for: is the whole game. A rule with no for: fires on a single scrape, so every deploy blip mails you. Requiring the condition to hold turns a noisy signal into a trustworthy one — and that Pending → Firing transition is exactly what you watch in the Prometheus UI.

    The five rules that actually exist

    AlertFires whenForSev

    Two of these catch what an HTTP-only alert structurally cannot: a pod that is down serves no requests, so a ratio stays quiet forever — and a capped agent run returns 200 with a useless body.

    Where a firing alert goes

    A separate topic from study reminders — on purpose

    Reusing the reminders topic would be one fewer resource and would mail every learner about a p95 latency breach — and mail the operator about flashcards. Different audiences, different retention of attention.

    The 2 a.m. loop — observability-mcp

    A second MCP server, stdio on a laptop, not deployed. Log search reads production in bulk; putting that surface in the request path buys nothing. Each step's output is the next step's input:

    1. check_alerts(env="prod") — names the rule and when it started
    2. query_logs_at(timestamp=<activeAt>) — the lines around that moment
    3. query_metrics_range(...) — whether the fallback absorbed it

    Fluent Bit ships gzipped NDJSON to S3, because a pod's logs die with the pod — and the failures worth investigating are the ones that killed it.

    Product signal, not just failures

    Accuracy is deliberately not a gauge. It's derived on the dashboard as a ratio of two counters, so it can be windowed over any range instead of being frozen at whatever the last write happened to be.
